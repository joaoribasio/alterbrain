# Getting started

Alterbrain is your second brain and writing twin. It keeps your notes, courses and projects, learns how you write, and helps with assignments, email, studying and job applications. It works whether you do an MBA, a university degree, online courses or a job with no courses. It lives in one folder on your computer, with a private backup online.

## 1. Open it

1. Open the **Claude** desktop app and go to the **Code** tab.
2. Open your Alterbrain folder (the one with `vault`, `system` and `config` inside).
3. Type in the chat box. Plain English is fine. There are no commands to learn.

If you installed Alterbrain by pasting the prompt from the README, close that session and start a new one in the same folder first. Alterbrain's safety rules only load when a session starts, so the setup chat must run in the new session. Do this once.

## 2. Run the setup chat (about 20 to 25 minutes)

Type `/onboard`. Alterbrain asks one question at a time. Early on it asks what you are learning or doing (an MBA, a university degree, online courses, or work with no courses), and sets the defaults from your answer. The essentials take about 24 minutes if you do an MBA or a degree, about 22 for online courses and about 21 if you work without studying.

| Step | What it's for | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Checks your computer, makes your folders, sets up the online backup (and offers to scramble your most private notes there) | 8 min |
| Identity | Gives your assistant a name and a tone you like | 2 min |
| You | A short profile and the facts drafts may use (a CV makes it quicker), and the question about what you are learning or doing | 5 min |
| Courses and projects | If you study: your programme (if you have one), courses, AI rules and deadlines (more if you hand over all your course files). If you work: your focus areas and projects | 3 to 6 min |
| Autonomy | How much Alterbrain may do on its own | 3 min |

Have ready: a CV if you have one, and anything you study from (a syllabus or course page, readings, slides). For each course Alterbrain asks for everything you have (slides, readings, Excel files, briefs). You can hand it over now, give only the syllabus, or say "later". If you do not study, there is nothing to prepare for that step.

You can stop any time. Say "later" and it saves your progress and adds a reminder to your task list. Say `/onboard` again to carry on.

Later, when you have time, there are optional steps: your writing voice, your career and job search, Gmail and extra tools, the look of your documents, and importing your existing files. When a new course or block starts, say `/course new`. Your course material counts too: download it from your school's or course provider's website and Alterbrain reads it (see [Bringing in your course material](bringing-in-course-material.md)).

## 3. Try your first requests

- "What is due this week?"
- "Explain <a topic you are studying> using my notes and readings." (If you have none yet, add a file first: "Add this PDF to my sources.")
- If you study: "Help me start my assignment for <course>."
- If you work: "Help me plan my project for next month."
- "Draft a reply to the latest email from <someone you write to>."

Not sure what to ask? Type `/menu`.

## 4. Know where things are

- **Your task list:** `vault/00_inbox/Tasks.md`. Alterbrain adds items when it needs you.
- **Drafts waiting for you:** `vault/00_inbox/outbox/`.
- **Your notes:** everything in `vault/`. Open it with Obsidian to browse (see "Using Obsidian").

## 5. Three promises

1. **It drafts; you send.** Nothing leaves your computer without your OK, unless you change that.
2. **It doesn't make things up about you.** It only uses facts you confirmed.
3. **Your work is saved and backed up automatically.** You never need to touch git.
