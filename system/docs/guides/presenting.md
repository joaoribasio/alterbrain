# Presenting: decks, documents and the delivery check

Alterbrain builds every report, deck, memo and workbook to the same standard. This guide is what to expect and what to ask for.

## Storyline first

Before it builds a single slide, Alterbrain writes the **ghost deck**: one line per slide, each a full sentence that states the takeaway. You read only those lines, top to bottom. If they tell the story on their own, the deck works; if not, the argument is not ready yet. You approve the ghost deck, and then the slides are built.

The rules behind it, in short:

- **Answer first.** The conclusion goes up front (an executive summary), then the support. Backup material goes in an appendix.
- **Situation, complication, question, answer.** The reader should feel the problem before they see the solution. The answer is not hidden at the end.
- **Grouped without overlap.** The reasons under a point do not overlap and together cover it (MECE).
- **Action titles.** A slide title is a sentence with a takeaway, at most two lines ("Prices rose 4% while volume fell 9%"), not a topic ("Pricing").
- **The titles alone tell the story; the body proves the title.**
- **One message per slide.**
- **Charts:** chosen for the message, the key number highlighted, labels on the data instead of a legend, a title that says what the chart shows, and a source line on every data slide. Numbers and units are the same everywhere.
- **Readable:** colour-blind-safe contrast and a minimum font size.

If your school's template or the assignment's rubric prescribes a structure, **it wins**. Alterbrain says so in one line and uses the story moves inside it.

## Telling it as a story

Inside the structure, Alterbrain uses a story arc by default: the stakes after the answer, a real person or customer moment if your sources contain one, concrete specifics (a number, a name, a date), contrast between what is and what could be, and an ending that returns to the opening. It never invents an anecdote or a fact. If it has no real moment to use, it writes the point plainly and asks whether you have one. For academic work the story is kept short and quiet.

All of it is in **your voice** (from your voice profile) at the **tone** you pick: academic, professional or conversational. Alterbrain suggests a default for your programme, course or project, and you can change it for one deliverable. For a group deliverable it asks once: "sound like you, or a neutral team voice?" If you have no voice profile yet, it says so once and suggests the voice setup.

## Reading deck or presenting deck

Decide at the start; the template's style sets the default.

| | Reading deck | Presenting deck |
|---|---|---|
| For | Being read without you there (a board pack, a graded submission) | Being spoken to (a pitch, a class presentation) |
| Slides | Self-explanatory, with an "In brief" box, sources and footnotes | Minimal text, one idea, a strong visual |
| Where the content is | On the slide | In the speaker notes |

## The rehearsal pack

When a deck is approved, Alterbrain offers a rehearsal pack (once; say no and it does not ask again for that deck):

- **speaker notes** in your voice, one per content slide, written into the deck;
- a **timing plan** against the slot you have;
- the **ten likeliest audience questions** with short answers, from the devil's-advocate review (an answer that the deck cannot support is shown as a gap to close, not a made-up answer);
- a **one-page cheat sheet**: the answer in a sentence, the numbers to remember, the order of the slides, the opening and closing lines, and the three hardest questions.

The speaker notes travel inside the PowerPoint file, so they follow the same rules as anything that leaves your computer: only facts you have cleared for outside use. The questions and the cheat sheet stay in your vault.

## Looking at every page

Before it tells you a file is ready, Alterbrain **looks at every page or slide itself**: the cover, the edges, half-empty boxes, fallback fonts, overlaps, small text. A helper saying it checked is never accepted as proof.

- **A PDF** is turned into page pictures with a small free tool called Poppler. Alterbrain offers to install it once, at the end of a health check or during setup, never in the middle of your work. See [Troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md).
- **A PowerPoint, Word or Excel file** is exported to a PDF copy through the program itself if it is installed and closed. PowerPoint, Word and Excel on Windows work this way. [Unverified] The Mac route has not been run on a Mac. If the program is not available, Alterbrain uses LibreOffice and warns you that fonts and line breaks may differ from your real file.
- Your original file is never changed by this.

## The delivery check

Last step before you upload. Alterbrain checks:

1. the cover or title slide is complete: names, programme, course, team, no placeholder;
2. file names follow the course or template rule and share a base name (for example `Team5_StrategyCase.docx`, `.pptx`, `.xlsx`);
3. every page has been looked at;
4. the workbook opens with calculated values and no error cells;
5. the report, deck and workbook agree on the headline numbers and terms;
6. the upload size is within the limit;
7. no working marks or placeholders are left in anything you hand in ([Inference], [Unverified], [FACT NEEDED], [Teammate name] and the like).

Then it adds a task for you to upload. **Alterbrain never uploads or sends your file.** If it could not check something, it says so in plain words ("LibreOffice export, so fonts may look different from the real file").

If your rubric contradicts one of the automatic checks, the rubric wins, Alterbrain tells you in one line, and it is written in the hand-over. The label check is never waived.

## Ask for a review

At the end of a deliverable, Alterbrain offers a critique once. You can also ask any time: "critique this deck". A **quick** panel (two or three reviewers) is the cost-aware choice on Pro; the **full** panel adds more reviewers and a final pass on the largest model. For a deck the reviewers include structure, production (layout and consistency across files) and the devil's advocate. For a workbook there is also a model audit (does every number in the report trace to a cell, are inputs kept apart from calculations, are there numbers hidden inside formulas). See [Models and costs](models-and-costs.md).
