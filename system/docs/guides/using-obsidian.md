# Using Obsidian

Obsidian is a free app for reading and editing your notes. Alterbrain writes the notes; Obsidian lets you browse, search and edit them. You don't need it, but it makes everything easier to see.

## Set it up (5 minutes)

1. Download Obsidian from **obsidian.md** and install it. No account is needed.
2. Open Obsidian → **Open folder as vault**.
3. Choose the **`vault`** folder inside your Alterbrain folder. (Not the Alterbrain folder itself.)
4. If Obsidian asks about community plugins, choose **Turn on community plugins**. Alterbrain's setup already placed two:
   - **Tasks**: shows your task lists on the Home page;
   - **Git**: an extra backup for edits you make in Obsidian.
5. Open **Home** from the file list on the left.

## Find your way around

| Folder | What's inside |
|---|---|
| `00_inbox` | Your task list, drafts (outbox), proposals, quick captures |
| `10_projects` | Assignments and other projects with a deadline |
| `20_areas` | Your courses and your career |
| `30_wiki` | Knowledge Alterbrain maintains: concepts, frameworks, companies |
| `40_sources` | Your imported files and a note about each |
| `50_learning` | Study cards |
| `60_people` | People you work with (business info only) |
| `70_journal` | Daily, weekly and decision notes |
| `80_me` | Your profile, fact sheet, voice and document style |

## Everyday use

- **Search:** press `Ctrl+Shift+F` (Windows) or `Cmd+Shift+F` (Mac).
- **Links:** `[[Porter's Five Forces]]` links to that note. Click to open it.
- **Tick a task:** click its checkbox.
- **Edit anything** in your notes. Alterbrain reads your changes next time.
- **Graph view** (left sidebar) shows how your notes connect.

## Habits that keep a vault useful

These ideas come from Steph Ango, the CEO of Obsidian, who has written about how he organises his own notes. I have paraphrased them; the originals are at https://stephango.com/vault and https://stephango.com/file-over-app (read on 2026-10-07). Alterbrain follows them where they fit.

- **Your notes are just text files.** Every note is a plain Markdown file that opens in any editor. If Obsidian disappeared tomorrow, you would lose nothing.
- **Write dates one way: 2026-10-07.** Year, month, day. It sorts properly and nothing is ambiguous.
- **Describe notes instead of filing them.** The small box at the top of a note (its "properties": type, status, course) says what it is. Alterbrain's views use those to build lists for you. So you rarely need a new folder.
- **Link as you write.** The first time you mention a concept, a company or a person, put it in double square brackets: `[[Porter's Five Forces]]`. A link to a note that doesn't exist yet is fine; it is a reminder of what to write next.
- **Keep other people's words separate from yours.** Articles and PDFs live in `40_sources`. Your own thinking lives in your notes, with a source for every claim.
- **Jot first, tidy later.** Say "note that…" and Alterbrain saves it as you wrote it. The weekly review turns the pile into proper notes.
- **Wander now and then.** Open an old note at random and add the links it is missing. It is how forgotten ideas find their way back.

One difference: Steph keeps most of his notes in one flat folder. Alterbrain uses the numbered folders above, because it finds your files by their place. Please keep them.

## Please don't

- Rename or move the main folders (`00_inbox`, `20_areas` and so on). Alterbrain looks for them by name.
- Edit files in `40_sources/raw/`. They are your untouched originals.
- Edit `80_me/SOUL.md`'s Boundaries section. Ask Alterbrain to change tone instead.

## Obsidian and Alterbrain at the same time

That's fine. If both change the same note at the same moment, the backup keeps both versions and Alterbrain helps you combine them.

## On your phone

Optional. You can use the Obsidian mobile app with Obsidian Sync (paid) or another sync method. Alterbrain itself runs on your computer.
