# What can I ask?

Ask in your own words. These examples show the range. Type `/menu` for the live list, including add-ons you can build.

## Study
- "Explain net present value with an example from my Corporate Finance notes."
- "Make study cards from this week's slides."
- "Quiz me on the cards due today."

## Assignments
- "Start my Marketing assignment. Here's the brief." (It checks the course AI rules first.)
- "Give me three possible main arguments for this case."
- "Critique my draft like a tough grader."
- "Turn it into a PDF that fits in 6 pages."

## Email (after you connect Gmail)
- "Draft a reply to the email from the programme office."
- "What did my study group agree on this week?"
- "Make this email sound more like me."

## Jobs in the Netherlands
- "Find strategy roles in Rotterdam from employers that can sponsor a visa."
- "Write a cover letter for this job ad."
- "Which applications need something from me this week?"

## Documents
- "Turn this note into a PDF with my colours."
- "Apply Porter's Five Forces to the Dutch grocery market."
- "Check this text for phrases that sound like AI."

## Your notes and files
- "Add this PDF to my sources." (Drag the file into the chat.)
- "What do my notes say about network effects? Show the sources."
- "Remind me to email my group on Friday."
- "Run my weekly review."

## Build something new
- "I keep summarising case readings by hand. Could you make that a skill?"
- "Show me what you can build for me."

## Tips for asking well

- **Point to the material.** Drag in the file, or name the course or note.
- **Say who it's for.** "For my professor" and "for a recruiter" lead to different drafts.
- **Say the limit.** "One page", "200 words", "by Friday".
- **For bigger jobs, expect a few questions first.** Alterbrain checks it understands before it starts (this is called *clarify*). Reply "defaults" to accept its suggestions.
- **Correct it.** "Shorter", "less formal", "use my own words". Say "remember that" and it keeps the lesson for next time.

## Answers with sources

When Alterbrain answers from your notes, it shows where each point came from, like `[Source: [[Lecture 3]] | 2026-10-01 | confidence: high]`. Anything it is not sure about is marked `[Inference]` or `[Unverified]`.
