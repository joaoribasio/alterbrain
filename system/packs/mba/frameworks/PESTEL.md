# PESTEL

**Definition:** A checklist of six outside factors that shape a business: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental and Legal.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it at the start of a strategy case or a market-entry decision.
- Use it when entering a new country or a regulated industry.
- Do not use it as a list of facts. It must show which factors matter.
- Do not use it for competitor analysis. Use [[Porter's Five Forces]].

## Inputs you need
- The business, the market and the time horizon (usually three to five years).
- Reliable sources: government data, regulators, industry reports.

## Step-by-step
1. Define the business and the time horizon.
2. For each of the six factors, list the current facts and the expected changes.
3. Judge each change: how likely is it, and how big is the impact?
4. Keep the top 5 to 8 factors. Drop the rest.
5. Say whether each is an opportunity or a threat, and why.
6. Link to a decision: what should the firm do about the top factors?

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Writing a long, generic list of every factor.
- No evidence, and no dates or numbers.
- Describing today only, with no view of change.
- Not ranking by impact.
- Mixing up Environmental (nature, climate) with the general business environment.

## Worked example (synthetic)
Business: an e-scooter rental start-up planning to launch in a Dutch city.
- **Political:** city council is capping licences at 500 scooters. High impact.
- **Economic:** students have low budgets, so price sensitivity is high. Medium impact.
- **Social:** growing preference for shared transport among under-30s. Medium impact.
- **Technological:** battery swaps now take two minutes, lowering running cost. Medium impact.
- **Environmental:** the city wants fewer cars, which supports demand. Medium impact.
- **Legal:** helmet and insurance rules may change. High impact.

Top two: the licence cap and the legal rules. The plan should start with a licence application and a legal check.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Which two factors could change the whole picture?
- What is certain, and what is only likely?
- Which factors differ by country?
- What would we monitor over time?
