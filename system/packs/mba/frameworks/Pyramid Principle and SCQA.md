# Pyramid Principle and SCQA

**Definition:** Start with the answer, then support it with a few grouped arguments, each backed by evidence. SCQA (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer) is the opening that sets up the answer.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it for any assignment, case write-up, executive summary or slide deck.
- Use it when the reader is busy and wants the answer first.
- Do not use it for a story you want to reveal slowly, such as a speech meant to build suspense.
- Do not start writing before you know your answer.

## Inputs you need
- A clear question from the brief or the client.
- Your analysis and key facts.
- The reader and what they already know.

## Step-by-step
1. **Write the question** the reader wants answered.
2. **Write the answer** in one sentence (the top of the pyramid).
3. **Write the SCQA opening:**
   - **Situation:** what the reader already agrees with.
   - **Complication:** what has changed or gone wrong.
   - **Question:** the question that results.
   - **Answer:** your one-sentence answer.
4. Group your **key arguments** (usually 3). Each one supports the answer.
5. Check the groups are logical: same kind of idea, MECE (see [[MECE Issue Trees]]).
6. Under each argument, list the **evidence** (numbers, quotes, examples).
7. Check "so what?" going up and "why?" going down. Both must work.
8. Write headings that are full sentences saying the point, not topics.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Building up to the answer instead of giving it first.
- Topic headings ("Market analysis") instead of message headings ("The market is growing 8% a year").
- More than five arguments at one level, or arguments that overlap.
- A long situation section that is not agreed fact.
- Evidence with no link to the argument.

## Worked example (synthetic)
Question: "Should Alex Doe's cafe open a second site?"
- **Situation:** The cafe has been profitable at its campus site for two years.
- **Complication:** Rent for the campus site rises 25% next year, and the queue limits sales at lunch.
- **Question:** Should the cafe open a second site?
- **Answer:** Yes, open a small second site near the station by March.

Support:
1. **Demand is there.** Station footfall is 9,000 people a day, and 30% of campus customers live nearby.
2. **It pays back fast.** Fit-out of EUR 40,000 is repaid in 14 months at expected margins.
3. **The risk is small.** The lease is two years with a break clause.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Can I say my answer in one sentence?
- Does each heading make a point?
- If a reader stops after page one, do they have the answer?
- Is each group of arguments complete and without overlap?
