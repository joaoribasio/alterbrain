# Value Proposition Canvas

**Definition:** A two-part picture. On one side, the customer's jobs, pains and gains. On the other, your products, pain relievers and gain creators. The goal is "fit" between them.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it to design or fix an offer for one segment.
- Use it before filling in the [[Business Model Canvas]].
- Do not mix many segments in one canvas. Do one per segment.
- Do not invent customer pains from your desk. Check them with real people.

## Inputs you need
- One clearly named customer segment.
- Interviews or observations of that segment.
- A list of your products and services.

## Step-by-step
1. Pick **one** segment.
2. **Customer jobs:** what are they trying to get done (functional, social, emotional)?
3. **Pains:** what annoys, costs or frightens them?
4. **Gains:** what would delight them or save them effort?
5. Rank jobs, pains and gains by importance.
6. **Products and services:** list what you offer.
7. **Pain relievers:** how exactly does the offer reduce each top pain?
8. **Gain creators:** how does it create each top gain?
9. Check fit: does every top pain and gain have an answer? Remove features that answer nothing.
10. Test with customers and revise.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Pains that are really missing features of your product.
- Trying to answer every pain, so the offer has no focus.
- No ranking.
- Claiming fit with no customer evidence.
- Confusing the value proposition with a slogan.

## Worked example (synthetic)
Segment: first-year students who commute to Rotterdam by train.
- **Jobs:** attend lectures, keep notes organised, spend little.
- **Pains (top 2):** forgetting kit on busy days; paying for supplies twice.
- **Gains (top 1):** feeling prepared without effort.
- **Offer:** monthly "StudyKit" box.
- **Pain reliever:** a kit that matches the timetable, so nothing is forgotten; a swap-or-refund rule so no one pays twice.
- **Gain creator:** a checklist card in each box.
Features with no link (a free pen engraving) are cut.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Which pain would make a customer switch?
- Did I check this with five real people?
- What do customers use today instead?
- Which feature can I delete without anyone noticing?
