# Kotter's 8-Step Change Model

**Definition:** Eight steps that large change efforts need, in order: urgency, a coalition, a vision, communication, removing barriers, short-term wins, keeping momentum, and anchoring the change in culture.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it for big, organisation-wide change (a new strategy, a merger, a digital move).
- Use it to explain why a past change failed.
- Do not use it for small, local changes. It is too heavy.
- Do not treat it as a strict recipe. Real change loops back.

## Inputs you need
- A clear description of the change and why it matters.
- A map of who is affected and who can help or block ([[Stakeholder Mapping (Power-Interest)]]).
- Current culture and structure ([[McKinsey 7S]]).

## Step-by-step
1. **Create urgency.** Show why change is needed now, with facts.
2. **Build a guiding coalition.** Gather people with power, credibility and skill.
3. **Form a strategic vision.** Say where you are going in a few clear sentences.
4. **Enlist a volunteer army.** Communicate the vision often, in many ways, and live it.
5. **Enable action by removing barriers.** Fix systems, structures and rules that block change.
6. **Generate short-term wins.** Plan visible gains within months and celebrate them.
7. **Sustain acceleration.** Do not declare victory early. Use wins to tackle bigger problems.
8. **Institute change.** Link the new behaviour to the culture, hiring and rewards.

(Steps 4 to 8 use the later wording of the model. The 1995 article names step 4 "communicate the vision" and step 5 "empower broad-based action". Check which version your course uses.)

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Listing the eight steps with no link to the case.
- Skipping step 1 and starting with a plan.
- Declaring victory too soon.
- No sign of how the culture will change.
- Treating all resistance as irrational. Some of it is useful feedback.

## Worked example (synthetic)
A Dutch regional hospital moves from paper to digital patient files.
1. Urgency: 12% of records had errors last year.
2. Coalition: the medical director, two senior nurses, the head of IT.
3. Vision: "One safe record for every patient by next September."
4. Communication: monthly open sessions and a ward champion on each floor.
5. Barriers: the old approval process is cut from five signatures to two.
6. Wins: the first ward goes live in month 4 with a 40% drop in recording errors.
7. Momentum: three more wards each quarter, training led by the first ward.
8. Anchor: digital record use becomes part of the nurse annual review.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Do people really feel urgency, or only the leaders?
- Who is missing from the coalition?
- What will people see in the first 100 days?
- What will keep the new way in place after the project ends?
