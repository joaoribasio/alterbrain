# BCG Growth-Share Matrix

**Definition:** A grid that sorts a firm's products or business units by market growth and relative market share into Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks and Dogs.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it to allocate money across several products or units.
- Use it to explain why a firm funds one unit with profits from another.
- Do not use it for a single product firm.
- Do not use it as the only tool. It ignores synergies, strategy and market definition problems.

## Inputs you need
- Sales and market share for each unit.
- The share of the largest rival in each market.
- Market growth rate for each market.

## Step-by-step
1. List each product or unit and define its market clearly.
2. Compute **relative market share** = your share divided by the share of your largest rival.
3. Find the **market growth rate** (annual, usually).
4. Choose cut-offs. Common ones are 10% growth and relative share of 1.0.
5. Place each unit on the grid:
   - **Star:** high growth, high share. Invest to keep the lead.
   - **Cash Cow:** low growth, high share. Harvest the cash.
   - **Question Mark:** high growth, low share. Invest to win, or exit.
   - **Dog:** low growth, low share. Fix, hold or exit.
6. Draw a cash-flow plan: who funds whom?
7. Check the result against strategy before you act.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Using absolute share, not relative share.
- Defining the market too narrowly, so every unit looks like a leader.
- Calling a unit a Dog without asking whether it helps other units.
- Treating the matrix as a final answer.
- Placing items by feeling, with no numbers.

## Worked example (synthetic)
A food group (cut-offs: growth 10%, relative share 1.0):

| Unit | Own share | Largest rival share | Relative share | Market growth | Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plant drinks | 27% | 15% | 1.8 | 15% | Star |
| Protein snacks | 8% | 20% | 0.4 | 12% | Question Mark |
| Classic biscuits | 30% | 15% | 2.0 | 3% | Cash Cow |
| Canned soup | 3% | 10% | 0.3 | 2% | Dog |

Plan: biscuits fund plant drinks (keep the lead) and a test of protein snacks. Review soup for sale.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Is the market defined as customers see it?
- Does high share really mean low cost here?
- Which unit helps others (shared brand, shared plant)?
- What will the cash cow do when growth ends?
