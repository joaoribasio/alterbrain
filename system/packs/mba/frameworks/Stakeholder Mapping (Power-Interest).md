# Stakeholder Mapping (Power-Interest)

**Definition:** List everyone affected by or able to affect a project, then place each person or group on a grid of power (ability to influence) and interest (how much they care).

## When to use / when not to
- Use it at the start of a project, a change or a group assignment.
- Use it to plan communication and to find risks.
- Do not use it once and file it. People move on the grid.
- Do not map only the obvious. Include quiet, powerful groups.

## Inputs you need
- A clear description of the project or decision.
- Names of people and groups affected.
- Your knowledge (or research) of their goals and influence.

## Step-by-step
1. List all stakeholders. Think wide: customers, staff, managers, regulators, suppliers, investors, community.
2. Rate each one on **power** (low or high) and **interest** (low or high).
3. Place them in the grid:
   - **High power, high interest: Manage closely.** Involve them in decisions.
   - **High power, low interest: Keep satisfied.** Do not bore them; avoid surprises.
   - **Low power, high interest: Keep informed.** Share news; listen.
   - **Low power, low interest: Monitor.** Minimal effort.
4. For key stakeholders, note what they want, what they fear, and their current stance (supporter, neutral, opponent).
5. Plan the action: who talks to them, how often, about what.
6. Review at each project stage.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- A list with no grid, or a grid with no actions.
- Forgetting a key stakeholder (regulator, union, quiet investor).
- Judging power by job title only.
- Treating a stakeholder as fixed over time.
- No plan to move opponents or gain supporters.

## Worked example (synthetic)
Project: a university plans a new student-housing building.

| Stakeholder | Power | Interest | Plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| City planning office | High | High | Manage closely: monthly meetings |
| Board of the university | High | Low | Keep satisfied: short quarterly update |
| Neighbours | Low | High | Keep informed: open evening, letter |
| Student council | Low | High | Keep informed, and ask for ideas |
| Local shop owners | Low | Low | Monitor |

Risk found: neighbours could raise objections that reach the planning office. The team moves them up with an early meeting.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Who could stop this project?
- Who is affected but has no voice?
- Who has changed position since last month?
- What does each key stakeholder need from us?
