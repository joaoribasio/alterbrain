# Ansoff Matrix

**Definition:** A two-by-two grid of growth options: market penetration, market development, product development and diversification. Risk rises as you move away from what you already know.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it to list and compare growth options.
- Use it when a firm asks "where should we grow next?"
- Do not use it to say whether the market is attractive. Use [[Porter's Five Forces]].
- Do not stop at the grid. Each option needs numbers and a risk view.

## Inputs you need
- Current products and markets, with sales.
- Customer and market data for new areas.
- Capabilities and cash available ([[VRIO]]).

## Step-by-step
1. Describe today's products and markets.
2. Generate at least one idea in each box:
   - **Market penetration:** existing product, existing market. Sell more to the same people.
   - **Market development:** existing product, new market. New region or segment.
   - **Product development:** new product, existing market.
   - **Diversification:** new product, new market. Highest risk.
3. Estimate the size, cost, time and risk of each idea.
4. Check fit with the firm's capabilities.
5. Rank and choose. Say what you will not do, and why.
6. Plan a first step and a signal to stop.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- One example per box with no numbers.
- Calling a new segment "existing market".
- Recommending diversification with no sign the firm can handle it.
- Ignoring cannibalisation (new product eats old sales).
- No ranking and no reasons.

## Worked example (synthetic)
A Dutch tea company with EUR 10m sales sells loose tea to cafes.

| Option | Idea | Extra sales (year 2) | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Penetration | Bundle deals for current cafes | EUR 0.6m | Low |
| Market development | Sell to German cafes | EUR 1.2m | Medium |
| Product development | Cold-brew bottles for cafes | EUR 0.8m | Medium |
| Diversification | Tea-flavoured snacks in shops | EUR 1.5m | High |

Choice: penetration now (fast, cheap) and German cafes next. The snack idea has the biggest prize but the firm has no shop channel.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Which box is the firm already in?
- Which option uses a real advantage?
- What is the downside if we are wrong?
- What would we need to believe for diversification to work?
