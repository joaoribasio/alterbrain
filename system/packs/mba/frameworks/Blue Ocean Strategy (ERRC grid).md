# Blue Ocean Strategy (ERRC grid)

**Definition:** Instead of competing in a crowded "red ocean", create an uncontested "blue ocean" by making competition irrelevant. The ERRC grid helps: Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it when an industry is crowded and firms look alike.
- Use it to redesign an offer, a product line or a service.
- Do not use it to describe a small tweak. A blue ocean needs a real change in value and cost together.
- Do not assume the "blue" lasts. Rivals copy.

## Inputs you need
- The factors the industry competes on (price, speed, features, service).
- How the leading rivals score on each factor.
- What non-customers want and why they do not buy.

## Step-by-step
1. List the competing factors in your industry.
2. Draw a **strategy canvas**: factors on the x-axis, level of offer (low to high) on the y-axis. Plot your firm and two or three rivals.
3. Fill the ERRC grid:
   - **Eliminate:** which factors the industry takes for granted should go?
   - **Reduce:** which should be well below the industry standard?
   - **Raise:** which should be well above?
   - **Create:** which has the industry never offered?
4. Redraw the canvas for your new offer. It should look clearly different.
5. Check three things: a clear focus, a different shape, a catchy tagline.
6. Test the cost: eliminating and reducing should pay for raising and creating.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Filling the grid with small changes.
- No strategy canvas, or one that looks the same as rivals.
- Creating features without showing the cost saving.
- Ignoring non-customers.
- Assuming the new market cannot be copied.

## Worked example (synthetic)
Budget hotels in a Dutch city (compared with the mid-range norm):

| Eliminate | Reduce | Raise | Create |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reception desk | Room size | Bed quality | Self check-in app |
| Restaurant | Decor | Soundproofing | Free breakfast box to go |

Cost logic: no desk and no restaurant saves roughly 30% of staff cost. Part of that pays for better beds and sound insulation. Target: travellers who want a good night's sleep, not a lobby.

## Questions to ask yourself
- What do all rivals compete on, and does the customer really value it?
- Who are the non-customers, and why?
- Does the new offer differ in shape, not just in size?
- Where does the money to raise and create come from?
