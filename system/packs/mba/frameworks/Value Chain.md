# Value Chain

**Definition:** A firm is a chain of activities. Each one adds cost and, if done well, adds value for the customer. Advantage comes from how the activities are done and linked.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it to find where a firm is strong or weak against rivals.
- Use it to decide what to outsource, fix or invest in.
- Do not use it for a whole industry. Use [[Porter's Five Forces]] instead.
- It fits manufacturers best. For services, adapt the labels.

## Inputs you need
- A list of what the firm actually does, step by step.
- Cost data by activity, if available.
- What customers value most.

## Step-by-step
1. List the **primary activities**: inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service.
2. List the **support activities**: firm infrastructure, human resources, technology, procurement.
3. Assign costs, or at least rough shares of cost, to each activity.
4. Mark which activities customers value most.
5. Compare with rivals: where are we cheaper or better?
6. Look for links between activities (for example, better design reduces service cost).
7. Conclude: which activities to protect, fix or outsource.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Drawing the standard diagram with no analysis.
- Treating it as a flow chart of departments rather than of value.
- Leaving out support activities.
- Ignoring links between activities, which is where advantage often hides.
- Never comparing with a rival or benchmark.

## Worked example (synthetic)
A small Dutch cheese maker, revenue EUR 100 per 100 sales, costs per activity:

| Activity | Cost |
|---|---|
| Inbound logistics (milk) | 20 |
| Operations (making, ageing) | 30 |
| Outbound logistics | 10 |
| Marketing and sales | 15 |
| Service | 5 |
| Support activities | 10 |
| **Total** | **90** |

Margin is 10. Ageing is the activity customers praise most, so the firm protects it. Outbound logistics (10) is high against a rival's 6, so it is the first to fix.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Which activities do customers truly pay for?
- Where do we spend most and add least?
- Which links between activities are unusual or hard to copy?
- What could a partner do better than us?
