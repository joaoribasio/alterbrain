# Porter's Five Forces

**Definition:** Five pressures (rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power) decide how much profit an industry can keep.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it to judge whether an industry is worth entering or staying in.
- Use it to explain why firms in one industry earn more than firms in another.
- Do not use it to analyse one company. It looks at the industry, not the firm.
- Do not use it alone for fast-changing markets. Pair it with [[PESTEL]].

## Inputs you need
- A clear industry definition (what product, which region).
- Names of the main rivals, buyers and suppliers.
- Market data: concentration, growth, margins, switching costs.

## Step-by-step
1. Define the industry boundary in one sentence.
2. **Rivalry:** how many rivals, how equal in size, how fast is growth, how high are exit barriers?
3. **Threat of new entrants:** what stops newcomers (scale, brand, licences, capital, access to distribution)?
4. **Threat of substitutes:** what else meets the same need in a different way?
5. **Buyer power:** are buyers few, large, price-sensitive, or able to switch easily?
6. **Supplier power:** are suppliers few, or is the input unique or costly to switch?
7. Rate each force low, medium or high, with one piece of evidence each.
8. Conclude: is the industry attractive, and what should the firm do about the strongest force?

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Listing facts under each force without rating it or drawing a conclusion.
- Mixing up substitutes (different product, same need) with rivals (same product).
- Analysing a company instead of an industry.
- Forgetting complements, or treating the forces as fixed rather than changing.
- Stopping at "high" or "low" with no evidence.

## Worked example (synthetic)
Industry: budget gyms in Rotterdam.
- Rivalry: **high**. Six chains, similar prices, low switching cost.
- New entrants: **medium**. Equipment is cheap, but good city-centre sites are rare.
- Substitutes: **high**. Free parks, home workouts, apps.
- Buyer power: **high**. Members cancel monthly and compare prices online.
- Supplier power: **low**. Equipment is a commodity. Landlords are the one exception: **medium**.

Conclusion: the industry is only moderately attractive. A gym should win on location and cut churn, because rivalry and buyer power squeeze price.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Which one force matters most, and why?
- Is the industry boundary too wide or too narrow?
- What would change a rating in five years?
- What can the firm do to weaken the strongest force?
