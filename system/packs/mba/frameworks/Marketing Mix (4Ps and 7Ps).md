# Marketing Mix (4Ps and 7Ps)

**Definition:** The set of choices a firm controls to reach its target customers. The 4Ps are Product, Price, Place and Promotion. For services, three more are added: People, Process and Physical evidence.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it after you have chosen a target segment ([[Segmentation-Targeting-Positioning]]).
- Use the 7Ps for services, such as cafes, gyms, banks and consulting.
- Do not use it to decide who the customer is.
- Do not list each P in isolation. The strength is in how they fit.

## Inputs you need
- Target segment and positioning.
- Customer needs and willingness to pay.
- Costs, channels and competitor offers.

## Step-by-step
1. State the segment and the positioning in one line each.
2. **Product:** features, quality, brand, packaging, service.
3. **Price:** list price, discounts, payment terms, and how it compares to value and rivals.
4. **Place:** where and how customers can buy (channels, locations, stock).
5. **Promotion:** message, media, sales and public relations.
6. For services add:
   - **People:** who delivers, how they are trained.
   - **Process:** how the service flows from first contact to follow-up.
   - **Physical evidence:** what the customer can see (premises, website, uniforms).
7. Check fit: does each P support the same positioning?
8. Check the budget and plan the first test.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Describing each P with generic text and no link to the segment.
- A premium product with a discount price, or the reverse, without explanation.
- Stopping at the 4Ps for a service.
- Ignoring cost and margin.
- Confusing promotion with advertising only.

## Worked example (synthetic)
A new Rotterdam bubble-tea stall aimed at students (positioning: "fun, fast, fair price").
- **Product:** 12 flavours, vegan options, a loyalty cup.
- **Price:** EUR 4.50 per drink, EUR 3.80 on the second cup.
- **Place:** a stall next to the campus entrance, plus delivery in the area.
- **Promotion:** student ambassadors, Instagram, a first-week free-sample day.
- **People:** trained students, friendly and fast.
- **Process:** order by phone, collect without queuing.
- **Physical evidence:** bright branded cups and a clean stall.
All seven choices support "fun, fast, fair".

## Questions to ask yourself
- Does each P say the same thing about the brand?
- Would a customer pay this price for this product in this place?
- What is the weakest P?
- For a service: what does the customer see and feel at each step?
