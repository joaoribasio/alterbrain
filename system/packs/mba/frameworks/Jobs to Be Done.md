# Jobs to Be Done

**Definition:** Customers "hire" a product to do a job in their life. If you understand the job, you see the real competitors and what to improve.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it to find new product ideas or fix a product that sells poorly.
- Use it when segments by age or income do not explain behaviour.
- Do not use it as a label for any customer need. A job has a situation and a goal.
- Do not rely on desk research only. It needs real customer stories.

## Inputs you need
- Interviews with recent buyers (and with people who did not buy).
- The story of the purchase: what happened before, during and after.
- A list of alternatives the customer considered, including doing nothing.

## Step-by-step
1. Pick a recent purchase or switch.
2. Interview the customer about the story. Start from the first thought of change.
3. Find the **situation** that triggered it.
4. Write the job: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."
5. Note the three layers: functional (what gets done), emotional (how I want to feel), social (how I want to be seen).
6. List the **forces**: push of the old way, pull of the new, anxieties, habits.
7. List the real competitors (anything hired for this job).
8. Turn findings into product or message changes.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Writing a product feature and calling it a job.
- Using a demographic ("busy mums") instead of a situation.
- Missing the emotional and social layers.
- Naming only direct competitors.
- Asking customers what they want rather than what happened.

## Worked example (synthetic)
A university cafe sells milkshakes. Interviews show many are bought between 8:00 and 9:00 by commuting students.
- **Job:** "When I have a dull train ride and an empty stomach, I want something filling and easy to hold, so I can get to my first lecture without being hungry."
- **Competitors:** bananas, bagels, energy bars, coffee, doing nothing.
- **Action:** thicker shake, lid with a straw hole, pre-order by app. Do not add new flavours, because that is not what the job needs.

## Questions to ask yourself
- What was happening in the customer's life just before they bought?
- What else could they have hired?
- What anxieties nearly stopped them?
- Is my job statement free of the product itself?
