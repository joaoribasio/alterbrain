# Unit Economics

**Definition:** The revenue and costs of one unit of your business. If one unit does not make money, selling more only grows the loss.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it for start-ups, new products, pricing and "should we scale?" decisions.
- Use it to find break-even volume.
- Do not use it alone to value a company. Use [[Discounted Cash Flow Valuation]].
- Do not choose a unit that hides costs (for example "per app download" when the cost is per active user).

## Inputs you need
- The unit you sell, and its price.
- Variable cost per unit (ingredients, delivery, payment fees).
- Fixed costs per period (rent, salaries, tools).

## Step-by-step
1. Define the **unit** (one cup, one order, one subscriber per month).
2. Price per unit = revenue.
3. List every **variable cost** per unit.
4. **Contribution per unit** = price - variable cost. Contribution margin = contribution / price.
5. Add acquisition cost per customer if relevant (see [[Customer Lifetime Value and CAC]]).
6. List monthly **fixed costs**.
7. **Break-even units** = fixed costs / contribution per unit.
8. Compare break-even with realistic demand. Test price, cost and volume changes.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Mixing fixed and variable costs.
- Forgetting small variable costs (payment fees, packaging, returns).
- Assuming costs fall with scale without evidence.
- Break-even with no check against demand.
- A single scenario, with no sensitivity.

## Worked example (synthetic)
"Bean There", a campus coffee cart:
- Price per cup: GBP 3.50.
- Variable cost per cup: beans and milk 0.80, cup and lid 0.20, card fee 0.05. Total **GBP 1.05**.
- **Contribution** = 3.50 - 1.05 = **GBP 2.45** (70%).
- Fixed costs: GBP 2,450 a month (pitch fee, equipment lease, insurance).
- **Break-even** = 2,450 / 2.45 = **1,000 cups a month**, or 40 cups a day over 25 trading days.
- If price drops to GBP 3.00, contribution is 1.95 and break-even rises to about 1,256 cups.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Is this the right unit?
- Which cost would hurt most if it rose 20%?
- Can I really sell the break-even volume?
- What changes with scale, and what does not?
