# Business Model Canvas

**Definition:** A one-page map of nine building blocks that show how a business creates, delivers and captures value.

## When to use / when not to
- Use it to explain a start-up or a new business line in minutes.
- Use it to compare today's model with a redesign.
- Do not use it as a financial plan. It has no numbers or timeline.
- Do not fill it in once and forget it. Treat each box as a guess to test.

## Inputs you need
- A clear first customer segment.
- What problem you solve for them.
- Rough ideas on cost, price and partners.

## Step-by-step
Start on the right (customer side), then move left (company side), then finish with money.
1. **Customer Segments:** who are we creating value for?
2. **Value Propositions:** what problem do we solve, what do we offer?
3. **Channels:** how do we reach them and deliver?
4. **Customer Relationships:** how do we get, keep and grow customers?
5. **Revenue Streams:** what do they pay for, and how?
6. **Key Resources:** what assets do we need?
7. **Key Activities:** what must we do well?
8. **Key Partners:** who helps us, and for what?
9. **Cost Structure:** what are the biggest costs?
10. Check consistency: does each value proposition match a segment and a revenue stream? Does cost match the activities?
11. Mark the riskiest assumptions and plan a test for each.

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Many segments at once, with one vague value proposition.
- Boxes that do not link (for example, a premium offer through a cheap channel).
- Revenue streams without a price or payer.
- Listing resources and activities that every firm in the industry has.
- No view on whether the model is profitable.

## Worked example (synthetic)
"StudyKit", a monthly box of study supplies for Dutch first-year students.
- **Segments:** first-year students at city universities.
- **Value:** the right supplies and tips, delivered, with no shopping.
- **Channels:** student unions, Instagram, direct delivery.
- **Relationships:** monthly email plus a chat group.
- **Revenue:** EUR 15 per box per month.
- **Resources:** supplier contracts, a small warehouse.
- **Activities:** curating, packing, marketing.
- **Partners:** stationery wholesaler, courier.
- **Costs:** EUR 9 per box (products and packing), plus marketing and rent.
Riskiest assumption: students keep paying after month three. Test: a three-month pilot with 100 boxes.

## Questions to ask yourself
- Which box is the weakest, and why?
- Does every value proposition have a segment who will pay?
- Which assumption, if wrong, breaks the model?
- How would a rival copy this?
