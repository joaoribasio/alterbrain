# Segmentation-Targeting-Positioning

**Definition:** Split the market into groups with different needs (segmentation), choose which to serve (targeting), and decide what you want them to think of you (positioning).

## When to use / when not to
- Use it at the start of any marketing or growth plan.
- Use it when sales are broad but weak, or when the brand is unclear.
- Do not skip to promotion before choosing the segment.
- Do not segment on easy facts (age, gender) when behaviour or needs differ more.

## Inputs you need
- Customer data: who buys, why, how often, how much.
- Market research or interviews.
- The firm's strengths and rival positions.

## Step-by-step
1. **Segment.** Group customers by what separates their needs or behaviour (for example benefits sought, usage, situation), not just by demographics.
2. Describe each segment: size, needs, how to reach, what they pay.
3. Check each segment is measurable, large enough, reachable, and responds differently.
4. **Target.** Score segments on attractiveness (size, growth, margin) and fit (strengths, rival strength).
5. Choose: one segment, a few, or many.
6. **Position.** Write a positioning statement: "For [target], [brand] is the [category] that [key benefit], because [reason to believe]."
7. Check it is different from rivals, true, and relevant.
8. Hand the result to the [[Marketing Mix (4Ps and 7Ps)]].

## Common pitfalls (what professors mark down)
- Segments that are only demographic and say nothing about needs.
- Choosing every segment, so the brand has no focus.
- Positioning that is a slogan, or that matches a rival's.
- No reason to believe.
- Segment sizes that do not add up to the market.

## Worked example (synthetic)
Rotterdam student lunch market, 2,000 students:

| Segment | Share | Students | Main need |
|---|---|---|---|
| Budget-focused | 40% | 800 | Lowest price |
| Convenience | 35% | 700 | Fast, near class |
| Health-focused | 25% | 500 | Fresh, balanced |
| **Total** | 100% | **2,000** | |

Target: convenience (largest segment the cafe can reach quickly, good margin).
Positioning: "For students between lectures, Quick Bowl is the lunch that is ready in two minutes and still fresh, because every bowl is prepared each morning."

## Questions to ask yourself
- What separates one segment from another in behaviour, not just labels?
- Can I reach them cost-effectively?
- Will they notice my positioning against what they use now?
- What am I choosing not to serve?
